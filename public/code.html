CASE STUDY 2024

Senior Workout Buddy: Empowering Active Aging

A 4-week design sprint exploring accessible fitness technology for the elderly, bridging the gap between clinical requirements and joyful movement.

calendar_today Sept - Oct 2024
person Lead Researcher: Mary Jensen

Problem Statement

"Many seniors face barriers to consistent physical activity, including complex interfaces and lack of motivation."

Our research found that existing fitness apps are often designed for younger, tech-native demographics. Seniors often struggle with small hit targets, low-contrast text, and a lack of immediate, high-consequence feedback during health events. Our goal was to create a low-friction, high-engagement exercise companion specifically optimized for the 65+ demographic.

emergency

CRITICAL NEED

Instant access to emergency assistance during workouts was a top safety concern in user interviews.

visibility

USABILITY GAP

74% of participants reported difficulty reading labels on popular health tracking applications.

Visual Baseline

Initial conceptualization started with high-contrast, oversized touch targets to prioritize cognitive ease and motor-skill accessibility.

4-Week Sprint Timeline

WEEK 01 check_circle

Discovery

Literature review on geriatric UI patterns. Stakeholder interviews with occupational therapists.

WEEK 02 check_circle

Ideation

Rapid wireframing of the "Safe Start" workout flow. Mapping critical touch targets for emergency logic.

WEEK 03 radio_button_checked

Prototyping

Developing interactive Figma prototypes. Integrating audio feedback cues for low-vision users.

WEEK 04 radio_button_unchecked

Testing

Simulated remote testing with senior cohort. Validating task completion rates and cognitive load.

Final Outcomes

The Accessible Core

The final UI uses a 48px minimum hit target, high-contrast Material Symbols, and a tonal color system that reduces cognitive strain while maintaining a professional medical aesthetic.

favorite HEALTH IMPACT

92% Engagement Increase

During preliminary focus groups, users felt more confident initiating physical activity using the simplified Buddy interface compared to standard health apps.
